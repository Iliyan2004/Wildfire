1Welcome to PAKFIRE

PAKFIRE answers four questions about a wildfire in one place: where it will go and when, how hot it will burn, who and what is in its way, and whether a fuel break or treatment would help. You place an ignition on a map of Pakistan, choose the weather, and PAKFIRE does the rest.

1.1 What PAKFIRE does

  • Simulates fire spread. A physical model (Rothermel surface fire, minimum travel time spread) runs on the GPU over the real terrain, fuel and hourly weather of the place, with ember spotting, crown fire and wind shaped by the terrain.
  • Counts who is exposed. People (WorldPop scaled to the 2023 census), houses by type, informal settlements, hospitals, schools and power lines in and near the burned area, with the time the fire reaches them.
  • Plans evacuation. Routes out of the fire's path and from the nearest fire station, over the road network, with the time each route is cut.
  • Tests mitigation. Draw a fuel break or a treated area and compare the fire with and without it.
  • Works from real data. Elevation, 10 m land cover, vegetation greenness, satellite fire detections and weather are loaded automatically.

1.2 The modes

Instant A quick look

Click a place, pick a weather scenario, and see in about a second how far a fire could run in 1, 3 and 6 hours. Good for comparing places and weather quickly.

Physics The full fire model

Runs the full model for up to 120 hours of real or typed weather. Gives the time of arrival and intensity everywhere, an animated 3D fire, exposure and evacuation, ensembles of many runs, and mitigation comparisons. A run takes seconds to a few minutes.

Discover Fires burning now

A tab inside Physics: districts ranked by NASA satellite fire detections, the strongest recent hotspots, and live monitoring of a place with automatic scenarios.

1.4 What PAKFIRE can and cannot tell you

PAKFIRE is a decision-support and research tool. It is good at comparing scenarios, ranking places and weather, and showing how a fire could develop. Please keep these limits in mind:

  • Results are scenarios, not forecasts. A run shows what happens for the weather and settings you chose.
  • Burned area is about right, shape less so. Calibrated on satellite-observed fires in Pakistan (2012-2026), the model gets the burned area to within about a factor of two on held-back fires, but the shape and direction of the burn are only slightly better than an equal-area circle.
  • Fuel is mapped at 10 m and simulated at 50-100 m. Small firebreaks such as paths and narrow fields are not seen.
  • Crown fire uses assumed canopy values. Pine forest can crown; the canopy figures are estimates.
  • Weather is for one point. Wind is shaped by the terrain, but there is no weather model behind it.
  • PAKFIRE does not replace official warnings.

1.5 Before you start

  • A modern web browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari) with graphics acceleration switched on; the 3D views use WebGL.
  • An ignition inside Pakistan: the data covers latitude 23.5-37.2 and longitude 60.8-77.9.
  • For live weather and satellite detections the server needs an internet connection; without it you can type the weather yourself.

3Placing the fire

Every run starts with an ignition point. An area is optional: it sets the size of the simulation window.

3.1 Place an ignition

  1. Open the Fire tab and click Place ignition.
  2. Click the map where the fire starts. Drag the point to move it.
  3. To start several fires at once, place more points: the extra ignitions are listed.

3.2 Search, upload or start from a detection

You doPAKFIRE does

Search a placeFlies the map there; then place the ignition.
Upload a fileA GeoJSON or zipped shapefile with a point (the ignition) or a polygon (the area).
Pick a satellite hotspot (Discover tab)Starts the fire at a NASA VIIRS detection of the last days.
Share linkLink copies an address that restores the ignition and the weather settings.

3.3 Area and window limits

The simulation window is a square around the ignition: from your drawn area, or ±25, 40, 60 or 100 km (cells of 50 to 100 m). The largest window is 200 km across; a larger drawn area is cut to it.

ImportantThe ignition must be in burnable fuel. On bare ground, water or a city centre PAKFIRE moves it to the nearest fuel within 600 m, or tells you there is none.

4PAKFIRE Instant

Instant shows, in about a second, how far a fire could run in 1, 3 and 6 hours from a point you click, for a weather scenario you choose. It is the same physical engine on a small 24 km window with the weather held constant.

4.1 Step by step

  1. Open Instant from the start page or the top toolbar (address /instant).
  2. In the welcome card, type a place, choose a scenario and click Show fire spread; or close the card and click the map.
  3. Change the wind, its direction, the humidity or the duration: the result updates by itself.

5PAKFIRE Physics

Physics runs the full model. You place the fire, describe the weather and the window, and PAKFIRE computes when the fire reaches every cell and how hot it burns there, then what that means for people and roads.

5.1 The Fire tab: weather and timing

With Set the weather yourself you type the wind, its direction (or click the compass), temperature and humidity; they are held for the whole run.

5.2 Window, rates and options

SettingWhat it does
Backend (Advanced)GPU first, GPU only or CPU. The result is the same.
Random seed (Advanced)Changes where embers land. Run again with a new seed after a run does the same.
Cell size (Advanced)0 chooses it from the window size.

5.3 Running the model

  1. Check the ignition, weather and window in the Fire tab.
  2. Click Run fire model. A progress bar shows the stage: scene, weather, fuel, spread, outputs.
  3. When it finishes the 3D fire view opens and the numbers appear under the button.
NoteRuns wait their turn: the Queue label in the toolbar shows how many are waiting or running, and can cancel them. Cancelling acts between stages.

5.8 Ensemble and burn probability

In the Fire tab, below the run button, Run ensemble repeats the run 12, 24 or 48 times with the wind speed, wind direction, fuel moisture, vegetation greenness and spread rate varied within widths fitted to observed fires. The map then shows the share of runs that burn each cell, and the panel gives the range of burned area.

5.9 The Mitigation tab

ImportantThe effect of a break or treatment is what you set. PAKFIRE does not know how well a real treatment performs.

6Discover and monitor

Live monitoring checks NASA's satellite feed around a point every 1 to 24 hours. When a new detection appears within the radius it runs a 24-hour scenario from the strongest one and can post a message to a webhook (Slack, Discord or ntfy). It is experimental. Detections are 375 m pixels and include flares, kilns and small burns.

7Understanding your results

7.1 Arrival, intensity and flame length

Time of arrivalWhen the fire front reaches each cell, in hours after the ignition.
Fireline intensityHeat released per metre of fire front (kW/m). Flame length follows from it.
Head spread rateHow fast the front moves where it moves fastest (m/min).

Spot firesNew fires started by embers ahead of the front.
Crown fire areaWhere the fire climbed into the pine canopy.

7.2 How far to trust the numbers

On 527 satellite-observed fires held back from calibration (2024-2026) the median burned area was within about a factor of two of the satellite value, with a tendency to over-predict. The overlap of the simulated and the observed burn was 0.30 on average, against 0.29 for a circle of the same area. Fires above 2000 m are over-predicted most. The full record is in the Technical Reference, Chapter 23.

9Troubleshooting and questions

9.1 Messages you may see

The fire did not spreadFuel too wet or sparse for this weather: try drier or windier weather, or another place.

9.2 Frequently asked questions

Why is my forest fire so small? Low-elevation broadleaf forest and moist fuel spread slowly in Rothermel's model; that matches most observed fires. Try the Extreme scenario.

Why does the result change when I run again? Embers land at random. The seed fixes it; the ensemble shows the spread of outcomes.

Can I run outside Pakistan? No: the fuel, terrain and population data cover Pakistan only.
